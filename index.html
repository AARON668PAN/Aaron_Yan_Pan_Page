About

I am a fourth-year MEng student in Robotics and Artificial Intelligence at UCL, working as a research assistant in the UCL Humanoid Robotics Lab with Dr Chengxu Zhou, and an algorithm intern at LimX Dynamics. My research is on generating whole-body motion for humanoid robots: making it expressive, controllable and executable on real hardware. At UCL I work on diffusion-based motion generation conditioned on affect and on expressive human-robot interaction, deployed on the Unitree G1. At LimX I build the sim-to-real data engine for humanoid loco-manipulation: diffusion-generated human-object interaction trajectories, RL whole-body tracking in Isaac Lab, and a vision-language-action policy trained on simulated egocentric data and deployed on the LimX humanoid.

Projects

  • PAMoR: affect as a control parameter for humanoid motion

    The same walk, generated with four different valence-arousal commands and executed on a Unitree G1. Affect is measured directly from the robot's kinematics, so any text-specified action can be performed in any emotional style, in real time, with the command editable while the robot moves.

    Project page

  • Intention-aware expressive gestures for human-robot interaction

    A person extends a hand; a vision-language model reads the gesture, infers the intention with a confidence score, and picks a response, which a diffusion motion model turns into a whole-body gesture executed on the Unitree G1 in real time. The same loop handles waves, defensive postures and celebrations, and falls back to a neutral pose when the input is ambiguous.

    Full videoPaper

  • Calf-integrated arms for quadruped loco-manipulation

    A manipulator built into each front calf of a Unitree Go2 lets the robot grasp at ground level and carry with all four feet planted, so the base stays free to walk while an arm holds the object. Here it picks a basket off the floor and delivers it to a table in simulation; a vision-language model sequences the skills for longer tasks.

    Paper

  • Person following for an agricultural robot

    AntoBot, spring 2025. YOLO detects people and an OSNet re-identification network keeps the lock on the right one; an EKF fuses vision with odometry so tracking survives occlusions, and a potential-field planner keeps the robot behind the person and away from obstacles. Runs in ROS2 on the real robot.
